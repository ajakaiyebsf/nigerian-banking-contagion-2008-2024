## What this project does

This project maps a time-varying DCC-GARCH interconnectedness network, with Delta CoVaR supplying the edge weights, across the five Nigerian domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs): First Bank, GTBank/GTCO, Zenith, Access, and UBA. On that network it tests a fiscal-monetary transmission channel linking Brent crude oil prices, Federation Account Allocation Committee (FAAC) disbursements, and CBN external reserves to banking-system connectedness.

Because government intervention leaves its own fingerprint on observable stress signals, the transmission model carries five binary institutional controls: the 2009–2011 interbank guarantee, AMCON operations, the multi-window FX regime, COVID-19 forbearance, and the June 2023 FX unification. Without these controls, what appears in the data as market stress could equally be the residue of policy; the dummies exist to keep that distinction clear.

## How to run

1. Clone or download this repository.
2. Install dependencies: `pip install -r requirements.txt` (Python 3.10 or later).
3. Open `nigerian_banking_contagion.ipynb` in Jupyter, VS Code, or Google Colab and **run all cells top to bottom**. The first cell carries every import the notebook needs, so it must not be skipped. All file paths are relative to the repository root.
4. Expected first result: a merged sample of 4,208 daily trading days, 4,193 of them complete across all five banks, spanning 2008-01-03 to 2024-12-31. The full network, transmission, and panel estimation follow.

The notebook is deterministic. `np.random.seed(42)` is set in the first cell, and repeated cold runs reproduce every reported figure to within floating-point tolerance.

## Data notes — read before citing any number

**`ngx_prices.csv` holds log returns, not prices.** The filename is historical. The notebook reads it directly as a return panel; no price round-trip is performed. This matters because it means no return is ever computed across the splice boundary between the two equity sources.

**The NGX workbook arrives under present-day ticker names.** NGX supplied the 2005–2012 history already mapped to current corporate identities — ACCESSCORP, FIRSTHOLDCO, GTCO, UBA, ZENITHBANK — rather than under the predecessor tickers in use at the time. The notebook therefore performs a straight rename to its own naming convention (`FIRSTHOLDCO → FIRSTBANK`, `GTCO → GTB`, `ZENITHBANK → ZENITH`, `ACCESSCORP → ACCESS`); it does not need to splice across the GUARANTY→GTCO, ACCESS→ACCESSCORP or FIRSTBANK→FBNH→FIRSTHOLDCO restructurings, because the source has already absorbed them. Users comparing against contemporaneous NGX tickers should be aware the history is presented on a present-day basis.

**Source splice.** The NGX segment (2008–2012) and the Investing.com segment (2013 onward) are joined at 1 January 2013, with no return computed across the boundary. The two sources are validated against each other on their 2012 overlap window: per-bank correlations of 0.992 to 1.000 with mean absolute differences at or below 0.0011. The notebook prints this validation on every run.

**The workbook extends to 2005.** Three years of pre-sample history (2005–2007) are present but unused, since the study window opens in January 2008. Extending the sample backwards to capture the 2004–2006 Soludo consolidation is noted as future work.

The FAAC series carries one caveat worth stating plainly here and not only in the report. Because the CBN Statistical Bulletin publishes FAAC only at annual frequency, each year's real total is distributed evenly across its twelve months. The genuine signal in this series is year-to-year; the within-year variation is a modelling convenience, not observed data.

A note on the 2024 FAAC figure. An earlier version of this repository estimated 2024 from five monthly disbursement reports and arrived at ₦31,236bn. That figure was taken from the **gross** Federation Account total rather than the amount **distributed** to the three tiers, and was therefore roughly double the correct value and inconsistent with the 2008–2023 series, which is on a distributed basis. It has been replaced with NEITI's published full-year actual of **₦15,260bn**, a 43.9 per cent year-on-year increase that matches NEITI's independently reported 43 per cent. The 2024 observation is consequently real rather than estimated.

## Results (real data, as of this commit)

- **Corporate-action scan**: 24 flagged outlier returns (absolute move above 12 per cent) across the full 2008–2024 panel. Sixteen cluster in the March/April dividend season and are treated; eight cluster in the 2008–2009 global financial crisis window and are retained as genuine crisis-period market moves.
- **GARCH(1,1)-t against GJR-GARCH(1,1)-t**: BIC prefers the symmetric specification for all five banks, including across the GFC episode. Univariate persistence sits at or near the integrated boundary, a known feature of thin markets and consistent with the 10 to 15 per cent zero-return share documented below.
- **DCC(1,1)**: α ≈ 0.038, β ≈ 0.904, α + β ≈ 0.942 — comfortably stationary, even though the univariate variances are not.
- **Delta CoVaR and regime centrality**: computed with real Brent, reserves, and FAAC as quantile-regression controls. Systemic centrality shifts materially across the four regimes rather than remaining fixed on one or two banks. UBA and FIRSTBANK dominate the earlier crisis regimes; GTB and ACCESS rise by the post-unification period.
- **Transmission VAR and Granger causality**: the clearest finding. CBN reserve changes Granger-cause banking-sector connectedness (p = 0.0006 at one lag, p = 0.0039 at two lags); FAAC and Brent do not, on the joint test. The corresponding coefficient is negative at one month — a rise in reserves reduces connectedness — with the orthogonalised impulse response peaking at roughly −0.96 percentage points two months after the shock. Read against Kaminsky and Reinhart's twin-crises framework, this is consistent with treating the currency and reserve channel as a leading indicator in its own right. Brent's second lag is individually significant (p ≈ 0.02) even though its joint Granger test is not.
- **HMM regime-validation check**: the original single-initialization, full-covariance specification failed to converge. This was traced to over-parameterization and corrected with diagonal covariance and ten random initializations.
- **Oil-exposure panel**: a genuine bug was found and fixed. The Wald test of slope equality previously returned a numerically degenerate statistic under entity-clustered standard errors, because there were exactly five clusters matching five free bank-specific parameters. Clustering moved to the time dimension, which resolves it. The panel still runs on synthetic oil-exposure data, so the Wald statistic is not reported as a finding.
- **Betweenness centrality**: returns exactly zero for every bank. This is not a bug. The Delta CoVaR construction produces a complete directed graph, and betweenness is mathematically zero for every node in a complete graph regardless of edge weights.

## Model scope boundary statement

The committed scope, as agreed with the capstone supervisor, comprises: the DCC-GARCH and Delta CoVaR network with eigenvector centrality rankings compared across regime periods; the FAAC/Brent/reserves fiscal-monetary transmission channel tested by Granger causality within a vector autoregression; and the five institutional dummy controls. The parallel market premium was dropped from the core VAR specification — not because it is uninteresting, but because the original scope was judged too large, and the premium, still synthetic at this stage, was one of the pieces agreed to cut.

The following remain explicitly designated future work, not yet implemented on real data: the bank-level crude oil loan exposure panel, the parallel-market FX premium early-warning variable, and three of the five robustness checks (Haver Analytics premium substitution, the formal Chow test write-up, and the maximum entropy bilateral network comparison). A stated limitation follows from the oil-exposure exclusion: without it, common-factor exposure and genuine bilateral contagion cannot be fully separated, and results are interpreted accordingly.

This model is a co-movement network. It does not, and cannot on public data, assert bilateral default-cascade probabilities — those require the supervisory interbank exposure data held inside the CBN.

## Known data limitations

- NGX equities exhibit thin trading, with 10 to 15 per cent of days returning exactly zero, and daily price limits of ±10 per cent that censor extreme single-day returns.
- CBN reserves has no source ticks for five months, February to June 2009, interpolated linearly. This gap falls inside the GFC regime.
- The FAAC series has no real within-year variation, for the reason given above. It is not yet possible to distinguish whether the null Granger-causality result for FAAC is genuine or an artifact of that disaggregation.
- **FAAC is deflated to real terms using a placeholder CPI series** (`USE_SYNTHETIC_CPI = True`), pending collection of the NBS consumer price index. The FAAC channel result is therefore provisional on two grounds: frequency and deflator. No other series depends on this.
- The Chow test for the June 2023 unification has only 20 post-break observations within the sample window, which closes in December 2024 — low power, and not yet a substantive finding either way.
- One corporate-action flag, UBA on 2015-09-10, remains unverified. It falls outside the dividend season with no explanatory calendar gap, and may reflect the JP Morgan GBI-EM index exclusion sell-off of that period rather than a corporate action. It is retained untreated pending confirmation.
- The connectedness index is a rolling 52-week window resampled to monthly. Its final observation, January 2025, is built from a single weekly point where every other month averages four or five.
